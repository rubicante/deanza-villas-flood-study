De Anza Flood Explorer

The coloured lines are the paths water would tend to follow across the terrain toward the selected property. They're computed from USGS elevation data. Brighter and warmer colours mean more upstream land drains through that spot.

Controls

  • Slider: hides channels that drain less than the chosen area. Slide right to see only the main washes, left to see small rills.
  • D8 / D∞: two standard ways of routing water. D8 sends each cell's water to its single steepest neighbour. D∞ can split it between two neighbours, which suits spreading flow on the fan.
  • 1m / 10m: the elevation detail. 1m shows fine channels; 10m is smoother.

Please read

This is not a flood map. It shows possible flow paths from terrain alone, with no rainfall, no pipes or walls, and no depths. De Anza sits on an active alluvial fan, where floods can jump to new channels. San Diego County and FEMA guidance treats the whole fan as subject to flooding, including areas with no line on this map. The drainage traced to a single property can look small because flow on the fan spreads out. That doesn't mean the property is safe.

For official flood zones, turn on Flood zones (FEMA) in the legend, or see the FEMA Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov).

Data
